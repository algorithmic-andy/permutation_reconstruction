This study investigates whether the computational difficulty of reconstructing a permutation
matrix from partial invariant information can be predicted using conventional regression
methods. Permutation matrices were generated by reshaping random permutations of
(1, . . . , 25) into (5 × 5) matrices, after which subsets of row, column, diagonal, and antidiagonal
sum and product invariants were independently observed. Each resulting constraint
satisfaction problem was solved using Google OR-Tools CP-SAT, and solver difficulty was
measured using the logarithm of one plus the number of search branches explored.
A dataset of 10,000 simulated reconstruction instances was used with a test-train split
of 80-20 to compare linear regression, ridge regression, and regression trees using a common
predictive evaluation framework. The invariant feature representation contained 24 predictors
consisting of observed sums and log-transformed products, with unobserved invariants encoded
as zero. The results showed that linear regression and ridge regression achieved similar predictive
performance, explaining approximately 33% of the variation in log solver branches, whereas
the regression tree performed noticeably worse. These findings demonstrate that partial invariant
information contains meaningful predictive signal while also suggesting that conventional
regression models struggle to fully capture the combinatorial structure that may dictate reconstruction
difficulty. Future work should investigate structured representations, including
graph neural networks and explicit missingness indicators, to encode the relational dependencies
inherent to permutation reconstruction.
